  • Price sensitivity differs ~3.2× across store types: Airport customers are least sensitive (median elasticity -0.65), College Area the most (-2.09). Uniform pricing leaves money on the table in both directions.
  • Raise where customers are captive: Airport, Highway (avg change +6.8%). Cut where they are price-aware: College Area, Residential (avg -4.5%).

Gradient boosting forecasts almost as well, but its price effects are badly biased toward zero — trees learn price from the few weeks it moved and regularise it away. A good forecaster is not automatically a good pricing model. The pooled model's 90% uncertainty bands contain the true value for 89% of pairs (well calibrated).

3 · Price sensitivity (elasticity)

Elasticity = % change in orders for a 1% price change, at today's price. Below −1 (left of the line), a price rise loses revenue; but because every order also carries food cost and delivery fees, a rise can still add contribution down to roughly −1.5 to −2. Because the model uses price relative to competitors, the cross-elasticity to competitor price is the mirror image: a 1% competitor rise lifts our orders by |elasticity|%.

5 · Sensitivity & scenario analysis

Tornado: the plan's value is most exposed to baseline volume ±10% and price sensitivity (90% range); no single driver turns it negative (worst case ₹57,180/week). Guardrail frontier: the current ±10% limit captures 64% of the gain available at ±25%; wider limits add contribution mainly through bigger rises at captive stores, at the cost of more orders and more extrapolation. Monte Carlo assumes estimation errors are independent across items; a common bias (e.g. all elasticities 10% too low) is covered by the tornado.

Cost pass-through: for a 10% food-cost rise, the optimal price rises by a median 109% of the rupee cost increase. Not repricing costs ₹194,153/week across the chain vs ₹188,698/week with repricing.

7 · Roll-out, monitoring and limits

  1. Validate with a controlled test before chain roll-out. Pick 4–6 high-confidence recommendations per store type; apply them in half the matched stores for 8 weeks; compare against control stores (difference-in-differences). Promote the price list only if the measured contribution gain is within the model's range.
  2. Stage large moves. Where the unconstrained optimum is beyond the ±guardrail, move in steps of ≤10% and re-measure.
  3. Monitor weekly: forecast error per store × item (alert if WAPE > 15% for 3 weeks), competitor price index, orders vs forecast after each change, delivery mix.
  4. Retrain monthly (or after any menu revision) with python -m pricing.cli train; artifacts are versioned by data fingerprint and timestamp.
  5. Known limits: items are modelled independently (no cannibalisation or basket effects), competitor reaction is a scenario not a forecast, long-run brand effects are not in weekly data, and estimates are extrapolations outside the historical price range (flagged by the confidence label).
